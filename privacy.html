Privacy

The tag apps: Switchboard, Placard, Mothball, Larder, Frostbite and Packrat.

They have no server of their own, no accounts, no analytics and no cookies. What you save goes to a Google Sheet you own, and nowhere else.

Your Google Sheet

Everything you save in the apps goes from your phone straight to your ledger: a Google Sheet in your own Google account, with a small script in it that Google runs (so Google's own privacy policy covers it). That's house manual pages, totes and build cards, shelf items and spice jars, what's in the freezer, battery packs and who borrowed them. Pinch, Cellmate, Home Assistant and the Pi's morning slip read and write the same sheet from your network.

Each app also keeps a copy on the phone, in the browser's storage, so it opens at once and works with no signal, with the sheet's address and key. Clearing an app's site data in the browser's settings takes it off that phone.

The key in the sheet's About tab lets whoever has it read and change the whole sheet. Connect links and Switchboard's Another device code carry it: treat them like a password.

The house manual is public

Anyone with one of its stickers, a QR card or the link can read every page of the manual, with no key (not which stickers are written or locked). So door codes and passwords stay off it. A tenant's phone keeps a copy to read with no signal.

A tenant's report

Report a problem sends what the tenant types: problem, service visit or question, what happened, their name and phone or email if they give them, and which page it's about. It's kept in the owner's sheet and emailed, from the owner's Google account, to the address in the sheet's About tab; Reply goes to the tenant's email if they gave one. Until it sends, it stays on the tenant's phone only.

Anything else

GitHub Pages serves the pages, like any website host, and its own logs may record that your browser loaded them. The typefaces come from the apps' own sites. Call, Text and Email open the phone's own apps. The code is open at github.com/junkdrawerworks, so you can check all of this. The rest of the junk drawer has its own privacy page.
